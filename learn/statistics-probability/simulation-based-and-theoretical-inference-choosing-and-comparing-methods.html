01Start with the question, not the algorithm

An operations analyst compares output under a new procedure with output under standard operation. A theoretical t calculation, a bootstrap interval and a permutation test can all be computed from the same six numbers. Should their answers match? The useful response begins by asking what each method is estimating or testing, what probability experiment supports it and what is being held fixed.

Theory and simulation are not opposing philosophies. A theoretical distribution can describe a model exactly, or approximate it asymptotically. Simulation can approximate that same distribution, a fitted empirical sampling distribution or a conditional assignment reference. Before comparing numerical results, identify which of these objects was actually constructed. Running more iterations cannot turn one object into another.

This guide integrates earlier ideas rather than deriving every method again. We use one operations context for an estimation question and a hypothesis question. You will calculate a difference of means, build intervals, simulate a pivot, enumerate bootstrap resamples and enumerate label allocations. You will then reconcile disagreements through assumptions, conditioning, extremeness, interval construction and numerical precision.

The intended reader has Calculus I and introductory statistics. Guides 28,35 and 38 supply sampling-distribution reasoning, earlier inference and permutation foundations; necessary notation is recalled locally. A pivot is introduced as a statistic whose reference law does not depend on the unknown nuisance quantities. Programming is optional: the tables and worked solutions support the main reasoning without executing code.

The central skill is methodological accountability. A report should explain why its reference represents the study, what an interval or p-value means, and which uncertainty remains. Agreement between two invalid methods does not establish validity, while disagreement between valid methods can reveal different information being conditioned on.

02Write an inferential contract before calculating

An estimand is the population or study quantity we want to learn about. Here let μN be mean output for the new-procedure population and μS mean output for the standard population. Define δ=μN−μS, measured in completed units per hour. Estimation asks which δ values are compatible with the observations under a specified procedure. Testing asks whether the observations conflict with H₀:δ=0 against a prespecified two-sided alternative.

For the main model-based comparison, stipulate two independent samples of size 3. Within each group observations are iid Normal, with possibly different means but a common unknown positive variance σ². These are assumptions for a constructed teaching case. Three observed values cannot establish Normality, independence or equal population variances. Similar sample variances are a calculation, not proof of an assumption.

The unit is an independently sampled operations unit observed over a comparable shift. If workers share a team or measurements repeat over time, that sampling description needs revision. Treating correlated rows as independent can invalidate both a formula and a simulation that faithfully reproduces the wrong formula's assumptions.

This contract concerns a difference between population means. Without random treatment assignment or additional identification assumptions it does not establish the causal effect of changing a procedure for the same unit. Later we separately describe how the numbers can be read under a randomized design. That scenario changes the justification and null; it must not be silently attached to the observational analysis.

Keep the contract stable while comparing methods. If an assumption changes, label that as a new analysis. Selecting whichever output crosses .05 is method shopping, not a principled comparison.

04Distinguish the estimate from its reference distribution

The observed estimate δ̂=4 is one number. Its sampling distribution describes how estimates would vary across repeated samples from the population model. A bootstrap distribution instead describes estimates across resamples from the fitted empirical distributions. A permutation distribution describes a statistic across allowed label allocations conditional on the pooled observations. These laws have different random objects even when their horizontal axes use the same units.

A null distribution adds a hypothesis: it describes a statistic under H₀. Our theoretical t reference is a law of a standardized discrepancy under δ=0, not a distribution of plausible δ values. Its horizontal axis is dimensionless. A bootstrap distribution of δ̂* is generally centered near the observed estimate 4; it is not automatically a null distribution centered at 0.

Write down what changes in each repetition. In parametric null simulation, new response values are generated from the null model and t is recalculated. In nonparametric bootstrap, observed values can repeat within each sampled group. In permutation, values remain attached to their identities and group labels change subject to the permitted allocation rule.

The computational word resampling covers several mechanisms. The number of repetitions B does not identify which was used. A statement such as 10000 simulations is incomplete unless it specifies source distribution, unit, transformation, statistic and comparison rule.

A useful reading test is to finish this sentence: conditional on what we have fixed, the next repetition generates ____. If you cannot supply that random object, do not interpret the resulting histogram as a sampling law or null reference merely because it has a familiar shape.

05Construct the pooled t pivot under its model

With independent Normal samples and common variance, combine the within-group variance estimates using their degrees of freedom. Each group contributes n−1=2 independent residual degrees of freedom, giving ν=3+3−2=4 overall. The pooled estimate is sp²=[2 sN²+2 sS²]/4=(2+2)/4=1.

The estimated SE of the mean difference is sp √(1/3+1/3)=√(2/3)≈.816496581. It is smaller than an individual-response SD because means average independent variation, but it incorporates uncertainty from both groups. Using only one group's SE would omit part of the contrast's variability.

T(δ₀)=(δ̂−δ₀)/[sp √(1/nN+1/nS)].

Under the stated model and δ=δ₀, T has Student's t distribution with 4 degrees of freedom. This is a finite-sample result, not a large-n Normal approximation. Its proof uses Normal sampling and the independent chi-squared law of the pooled variance; those advanced distributional results are recalled, not rederived here. The ratio cancels the unknown common location and scale, making it a pivot for inference on δ.

06Estimate δ with a model-based interval

For a two-sided 95% interval, the t₄ critical value is c=t.975,4≈2.776445105. The central pivot event P(−c≤T(δ)≤c)=.95 can be rearranged because the SE is positive. It becomes P(δ̂−cSE≤δ≤δ̂+cSE)=.95 across repeated samples from the stipulated model.

Substitute the observed values: margin=2.776445105×.816496581≈2.266957936. The interval is [4−2.266957936,4+2.266957936]=[1.733042064,6.266957936] units per hour. The estimate 4 and uncertainty width should be reported together.

A Normal plug-in interval would use 1.959963985 instead, giving [2.399696108,5.600303892]. It is narrower because it replaces the small-sample t critical value with a Normal one while still estimating σ. Under our n=3-per-group unknown-variance model it is not the exact 95% construction. A visually convenient formula does not remove the uncertainty of estimating the scale.

Later the plot adds the exact empirical-bootstrap percentile interval [2.666666667,5.333333333]. The word exact there describes enumeration of that empirical resampling law, not exact 95% population coverage. The shortest interval is not automatically the most informative reliable procedure.

The frequentist confidence statement belongs to the procedure over repeated samples. After observing this interval, δ is fixed but unknown; the 95% label is not a posterior probability that δ lies between the endpoints. Nor is the interval a prediction interval for one future unit's output. Keep the estimand and the type of uncertainty attached to the bounds.

07Calculate a theoretical tail and match its interval

For the planned two-sided hypothesis H₀:δ=0, a large absolute t discrepancy is extreme. The p-value is P₀(|T₄|≥4.898979486)=2 P(T₄≥4.898979486)≈.008049893. Symmetry of the t law justifies doubling this upper tail. The hypothesis, distribution and direction must be declared before interpreting the number.

At α=.05 this t procedure rejects the equal-mean null under the independent Normal/common-variance model. Its corresponding 95% t interval excludes 0. That agreement is expected: both use the same pivot, critical value, two-sided convention and scale estimate. Solving |(δ̂−δ₀)/SE|≤c for δ₀ recovers the interval from the family of nonrejected tests.

A standard Normal tail at the same numerical statistic would be about .000000963, far smaller. The t₄ reference has heavier tails to account for the random estimated scale. This is a reference-distribution discrepancy, not Monte Carlo error. More simulation from a Normal reference would approximate the wrong target for this small-sample t analysis more precisely.

The p-value does not state that the null has probability .00805, that every unit benefits or that the procedure caused the difference. It is a tail probability under a declared model and null. Three observations per group leave model assumptions difficult to assess; a small model-based p-value cannot substitute for evidence about sampling and measurement.

When comparing any interval and test, first ask whether they are a matched construction. A percentile bootstrap interval and a label-permutation p-value are not automatically dual. Their disagreement at a threshold need not imply an arithmetic error.

08Simulate the same pivot instead of changing the model

To approximate the theoretical t tail by Monte Carlo, generate two independent groups of 3 standard Normal values under the null. For every simulated study, calculate both means, both sample variances, the pooled variance and the t statistic. Compare its absolute value with the observed 4.898979486.

Why can the generator use mean 0 and SD 1 when the real common mean and scale are unknown? Under H₀, write X=μ+σZ in each group. Subtracting group means cancels μ; the numerator contains a factor σ. The pooled sample SD also contains σ, so the ratio cancels it. The simulated t law is therefore the same for every μ and σ>0 in this model.

This is a pivotal construction, not an arbitrary fitted-null bootstrap. If a statistic's null law still depends on nuisance parameters, plugging in their estimates does not automatically create a finite-sample exact Monte Carlo test. It may be a useful approximation, but its justification and limitations must be examined separately.

Regenerate the denominator on every repetition. Dividing all simulated mean differences by the observed .816496581 would hold scale fixed and fail to reproduce the t pivot. In standard Normal coordinates the generated differences have their own random estimated scales. The null experiment consists of the complete analysis pipeline, not just a new numerator.

The simulated values around 0 are standardized generator inputs, not a claim that physical output can be negative in the operations process. Normality is the stipulated mathematical model; its substantive plausibility in a real application remains a separate question. Here simulation checks numerical agreement with a known reference, while theory explains why that reference applies.

09Measure numerical uncertainty without confusing it with sampling

Let B be the number of independently generated null studies, and r the count with |Tb|≥|Tobs|. Conditional on the observed threshold, r has Binomial(B, pexact) law under the correctly simulated pivot. The raw fraction p̂=r/B estimates the theoretical tail, with Monte Carlo SE approximately √[p̂(1−p̂)/B].

For a calibrated Monte Carlo rank test, include the observed statistic alongside the B simulated statistics and use (r+1)/(B+1) with inclusive ties. Here the observed null pivot and simulated null pivots have the same law, giving the necessary exchangeability. The corrected rank value and the unbiased raw estimator are related but distinct objects.

10Bootstrap the fitted empirical populations for estimation

For estimation, the nonparametric bootstrap fits each population by its empirical distribution. The new-procedure empirical population gives probability 1/3 to 8,9,10; standard gives 1/3 to 4,5,6. Draw 3 values with replacement independently from each empirical population and calculate δ̂*=x̄N*−x̄S*. Repeat to approximate its conditional distribution.

A new-group resample [8,8,10] has mean 26/3, while a standard resample [4,5,5] has mean 14/3, so δ̂*=4. Repeated values are intentional: they represent sampling from fitted discrete populations. They are not reassignment of the six original physical units to treatment.

Each ordered group resample has 3³=27 possibilities. Two independent group resamples therefore give 27×27=729 equally likely ordered pairs. Different ordered pairs can yield the same statistic. Keep their multiplicities when summarizing the distribution; do not assign equal probabilities to distinct difference values.

This bootstrap law is centered at the observed difference 4, because its empirical group means are 9 and 5. It approximates sampling uncertainty by replacing the unknown populations with fitted empirical ones. With only 3 values per group that replacement is a substantial approximation: unseen tails and rare outcomes are absent from the empirical model.

We enumerate all 729 resamples to remove Monte Carlo error from this specific bootstrap law. Doing so does not remove the statistical approximation of using empirical populations. This distinction lets us compare a perfectly computed bootstrap interval with a model-exact t interval without confusing numerical completeness with confidence coverage.

12Do not obtain a null test by misreading an estimation bootstrap

Every value of our empirical-bootstrap difference lies between 2 and 6. Counting how many are at most 0 would therefore give 0 out of 729. That is not the theoretical two-sided p-value and is not an exact label-permutation p-value. The resampling mechanism retained the observed group separation instead of imposing a null of no population difference.

A p-value compares an observed discrepancy with a null reference. An ordinary estimation bootstrap approximates variation around the fitted observed distributions. Its centering and construction are different. A zero count here tells us about the support of this fitted empirical law, not that equality of population means has zero probability.

Bootstrap hypothesis testing is possible, but it requires a suitable null construction or pivot and appropriate calibration. For example, a centered residual bootstrap or a bootstrap-t method must specify how the null is imposed, which nuisance parameters are estimated and what is recomputed. Simply subtracting a number and using an unexplained tail is not a general proof of validity.

Even an interval that excludes 0 is not automatically dual to every available test. Our exact empirical-percentile interval excludes 0, while the absolute permutation test gives .10. They use different constructions and do not form a guaranteed dual pair. The discrepancy should prompt a methodological explanation before a software investigation.

Checkpoint: what would changing B do to the bootstrap zero count? It could only approximate the same support more thoroughly. It cannot introduce a null experiment that the chosen generator never represented. The first repair is conceptual: define the hypothesis and the reference law, then select a defensible test.

In the observational interpretation, the justification is identical-distribution exchangeability under H₀: FN=FS. Conditional on the pooled values and sample sizes, labels are equiprobable. Outside that model, equal means alone need not imply equal distributions or exchangeability. A raw mean-difference permutation test is not universally exact for a weak equal-mean null with unequal variances or dependence. Those caveats remain even if all 20 allocations have been computed perfectly.

The finite reference has a two-sided floor .10 when the extreme complements are unique. It cannot reject at .05 for any allocation of this pooled list. More Monte Carlo draws would only approximate the same finite reference; they would not reproduce the theoretical t p-value by increasing precision.

14Explain why two valid exact tests can disagree

Under the stipulated equal-variance Normal model, the t test has an exact unconditional t₄ reference for the null mean contrast. The permutation test is conditionally exact or conservative after fixing the pooled observations. Both can be valid under the same Normal null while producing .008049893 and .10 for this sample. Exactness refers to calibration under a specified repetition scheme, not uniqueness of a p-value.

The t procedure uses more model structure and averages its null error over new samples, including new pooled values and new estimates of scale. The permutation procedure conditions on this particular set of six pooled outcomes and varies only labels. It has a coarse 20-point assignment reference. These are different probability experiments.

An unconditional level guarantee does not require the t procedure to have size≤.05 within every possible fixed pooled configuration. Conversely, conditional permutation validity implies unconditional validity under the corresponding model, but its discreteness can make it conservative. More conditioning can trade away sensitivity in this tiny design; it is not automatically a universal improvement.

Changing from raw difference D to a recomputed pooled t statistic does not remove the issue here. With equal group sizes, pooled total squared variation is fixed across labels; between-group variation grows with D², so the pooled within-group variance falls as |D| grows. Consequently |t| is monotone in |D| for these nondegenerate allocations. The same two extreme allocations form the permutation tail.

Report which assumptions justify the primary procedure and why it was chosen. Do not average the two p-values, label the smaller one correct by preference or label the larger one a failed algorithm. Their discrepancy is evidence about the methods' constructions, not a direct estimate of model misspecification.

For these six values, total squared deviations from pooled mean 7 sum 28. Between-group sum of squares is(3/2) D², leaving within-group sum 28−(3/2) D². Thus SE²=(2/3)[28−(3/2) D²]/4=14/3−D²/4. As D² increases, the numerator of t² increases and its positive denominator decreases. This verifies the ordering directly without claiming that all choices of studentization always preserve permutation ranks.

15Separate a randomized-design interpretation from a population model

Now consider a different study description: these six units were fixed in advance and exactly 3 were assigned uniformly to the new procedure. Under Fisher's sharp no-effect null, every unit has the same potential response under either assignment. The same 20-label calculation is then a design-based randomization test, with absolute p=.10.

The numerical table is unchanged, but its scientific justification has changed. Random assignment supplies allocation probabilities; the sharp null supplies missing potential responses so outcomes can be held fixed. This does not require the six outcomes to be a Normal iid sample. It does require the actual assignment protocol, consistency and appropriate treatment of interference.

The finite-study average treatment effect is a different target from a contrast of two observational population means. A sharp no-effect null is stronger than average effect 0. Heterogeneous positive and negative effects can cancel on average while violating the sharp null. Do not attach an exact average-effect interpretation to the sharp-null test solely because its statistic is a mean difference.

Our earlier t interval was justified by two independent Normal samples with common variance. It is not automatically an exact design-based confidence interval for that finite-study causal average. A design-based interval or a test inversion needs its own construction and assumptions; a constant-effect sharp-null family would be another stated restriction.

This parallel reading is included to teach why a method name and dataset cannot determine the inference alone. When interpreting a real study, establish which account of sampling and assignment actually occurred. You may compare assumptions transparently, but you cannot retrofit randomization after seeing an observational contrast.

16Name the layer at which a result is exact

The word exact needs a qualifier. Model exactness means a reference law holds at the stated finite sample size under a model, as with our Normal/common-variance t pivot. Design exactness means reference probabilities follow the actual assignment mechanism under a sufficient null. Numerical enumeration means every element of a chosen finite reference has been counted. These claims address different layers.

Our 729-point empirical-bootstrap law is numerically exact when fully enumerated. Its fitted empirical populations still approximate unknown populations, and its percentile interval can have imperfect coverage. A Monte Carlo t tail has numerical uncertainty even though the target t law is model exact. A completely enumerated permutation can be scientifically invalid if labels were not exchangeable or randomized as declared.

An asymptotic approximation is another layer: it becomes accurate under limiting conditions, usually involving increasing independent information and regularity assumptions. A large row count does not guarantee those conditions if rows share teams or long dependence. Nor does an arbitrary rule such as n≥30 establish every tail approximation.

For our main result, say the pooled t reference is exact under the stipulated Normal/common-variance model; the simulated version approximates its tail; the permutation law is fully enumerated conditional on the pooled data; the bootstrap percentile interval is an approximate inferential construction with an exactly enumerated empirical law.

Checkpoint: which error does increasing B reduce? Numerical simulation error. Which error requires improving a model, design or sample? Statistical approximation and scientific misspecification. Naming the layer prevents a large computation budget from being mistaken for a stronger inferential guarantee.

17Evaluate interval coverage in repeated simulated studies

Coverage is a property of an interval procedure across new datasets. To study it in a controlled experiment, generate M=2000 independent studies from two Normal(0,1) groups of size 3; the true contrast is δ=0. For each study construct the pooled t interval and a percentile bootstrap interval using K=499 within-group resamples. Count how often each interval contains 0.

With NumPy's stated generator and seed 394, the code below obtains 1896 t intervals containing 0, a rate .948; the percentile procedure contains 0 in 1635 studies, a rate .8175. These are simulation results for this declared model, sample size, quantile convention and finite bootstrap budget. They are not claims about every population or every bootstrap method.

The t procedure has theoretical coverage .95 under this model. Across 2000 independent studies, the expected simulation SE near .95 is √(.95×.05/2000)≈.004873. The observed .948 is compatible with ordinary outer Monte Carlo variation. The much lower percentile rate warns against treating an exactly calculated or heavily simulated percentile interval as automatically well calibrated at n 3.

There are two simulation budgets. M controls precision of the estimated coverage rate. K controls approximation of each study's bootstrap interval; its finite value is part of the procedure being evaluated. Increasing M does not remove finite-K endpoint noise, and increasing K does not make 2000 studies an infinite coverage experiment.

This experiment is a diagnostic, not a proof of general validity or failure. A broader assessment would vary population shape, heteroscedasticity, sample size, dependence and interval method. Do not call .8175 the exact coverage of the fully enumerated 729-resample interval: that empirical space belongs to the original illustrative data, while this experiment generates new samples and uses 499 draws per study.

18Change assumptions before changing numerical precision

Sensitivity analysis asks how conclusions depend on choices that matter scientifically. If the new group has a much larger population variance, the common-variance t model changes. A Welch procedure estimates each variance separately and uses a different reference approximation. It is not the same pooled test with more simulation. With our equal sample variances and balanced sizes, numerical coincidence would not establish universal equivalence.

If the populations are strongly skewed or heavy tailed, three observations cannot represent the tail well. A Normal model may be questionable, and the empirical bootstrap may also omit the important tail. Reject the false choice that removing Normality leaves no assumptions. Both approaches require a defensible sampling structure and sufficient information for their intended approximation.

If there are matched pairs, resample pairs for estimation and use an appropriate within-pair randomization or symmetry argument for testing. If treatment was assigned to teams, preserve whole-team labels. If responses are a time series, arbitrary row resampling breaks serial dependence. Use a design-compatible method or a justified dependence model rather than increasing B on the wrong independent-row generator.

Outliers also require a scientific decision. Replacing a mean contrast with a median or trimmed mean changes the estimand or sensitivity of the statistic. That may be appropriate, but it should be motivated and reported. Removing a value solely because a preferred method then rejects changes the procedure through outcome-driven selection.

A useful sensitivity report distinguishes three changes: same method with tighter numerical precision; same target under alternative plausible assumptions; and a different target or study design. Keeping those categories explicit makes a comparison interpretable rather than a collection of unrelated p-values.

19Choose a primary method through an explicit decision sequence

Begin with the scientific objective: population contrast, finite causal effect, prediction or another target. Our main contract asks about δ, a contrast of two population means. The next question is what created the data: independent sampling, matched pairs, clustered assignment or temporal dependence. This determines which repetitions can represent uncertainty.

Assess the assumptions supporting a theoretical pivot or approximation. If the independent Normal/common-variance model is substantively defensible, the pooled t construction provides exact finite-model inference and a matched interval/test pair. Simulation of that same pivot is useful for verification or extension, but is not required merely because computation is available.

If the theoretical law is difficult but a trustworthy null generator exists, Monte Carlo may approximate its tail. If estimation uncertainty is the objective and empirical resampling is justified, choose a bootstrap interval method and check its limitations at the available sample size. If randomized allocation or null exchangeability supports relabeling, permutation supplies a conditional test. These choices have distinct entry conditions.

Use the primary method chosen from the design and target, with sensitivity analyses that clarify assumptions. Do not select by interval width, whether zero is excluded or which p-value is smallest. A comparison can reasonably conclude that the available data support only a cautious descriptive result until better independent information is collected.

Before reporting, ask what evidence could change the choice: an assignment protocol, known pairing, a plausible variance difference, a heavy-tailed process or a material decision threshold. That question makes the decision sequence applicable in real work rather than a memorized list of software functions.

20Diagnose a disagreement in a reproducible order

First verify that both calculations use the same observations, missing-data rules, group labels and units. A minutes-versus-seconds mismatch or reversed subtraction can create a disagreement before inference begins. Next compare target and null: equality of means, equality of distributions and no effect for every unit are not generally interchangeable.

Then compare the statistic and everything recomputed with it. A mean difference, pooled t, Welch t and a rank statistic can order outcomes differently. Compare reference laws and conditioning: new population samples, fitted empirical resamples and conditional label allocations produce different experiments. Only after these checks compare tails, ties, interval definitions and quantiles.

If the contracts are truly the same, quantify numerical error. A Monte Carlo estimate within a plausible simulation error band of its theoretical target is not evidence of a faulty distribution routine. A difference that persists with easy exact enumeration or much tighter numerical precision needs a conceptual or implementation explanation.

For our case, theoretical t versus pivotal Monte Carlo is primarily a numerical comparison. Theoretical t versus permutation is primarily a conditioning/reference comparison. The t interval versus empirical-percentile interval differs by inferential construction and assumptions. Bootstrap support excluding 0 versus a zero null p-value is a misuse of an estimation law.

Finally inspect software defaults: equalvar, paired resampling, alternative, percentile/basic/BCa, inverse-CDF versus interpolated quantiles and random generator. Record explicit settings. Do not begin by blaming the library, and do not conclude that matching numbers prove the design is valid. A reproducible audit explains both agreement and disagreement through the probability experiment actually implemented.

21Reproduce the theoretical, bootstrap and permutation calculations

Predict the main results before executing: observed difference 4, pooled variance 1, t statistic 4.898979486, t p about .00805, t interval about[1.733,6.267], bootstrap percentile interval[8/3,16/3] and permutation p .10. The program uses Python 3 and SciPy; the resampling enumeration itself uses the standard library. The recorded environment uses SciPy 1.17.0, while the linked current documentation was 1.18.0. No identity of versions is assumed.

from itertools import product, combinations
from collections import Counter
from fractions import Fraction
from math import sqrt
import scipy
from scipy.stats import t, norm

new = [8,9,10]
standard = [4,5,6]
difference = sum(new)/3-sum(standard)/3
variance = lambda x: sum((v-sum(x)/len(x))**2 for v in x)/(len(x)-1)
pooled = (2*variance(new)+2*variance(standard))/4
se = sqrt(pooled*(1/3+1/3))
statistic = difference/se
critical = t.ppf(.975,4)
print(f"difference={difference:.6f}; pooled variance={pooled:.6f}; SE={se:.6f}")
print(f"t={statistic:.9f}; df=4; p={2*t.sf(abs(statistic),4):.9f}")
print(f"t95=[{difference-critical*se:.9f},{difference+critical*se:.9f}]")
print(f"Normal plug-in95=[{difference-norm.ppf(.975)*se:.9f},{difference+norm.ppf(.975)*se:.9f}]")
boot = Counter(sum(a)-sum(b) for a in product(new,repeat=3)
               for b in product(standard,repeat=3))
def exact_quantile(p):
    cumulative = 0
    for num,count in sorted(boot.items()):
        cumulative += count
        if Fraction(cumulative,729)>=p:
            return Fraction(num,3)
lo,hi = exact_quantile(Fraction(1,40)),exact_quantile(Fraction(39,40))
print(f"bootstrap ordered samples={sum(boot.values())}; percentile95=[{float(lo):.9f},{float(hi):.9f}]")
print(f"bootstrap variance={sum(Fraction(c,729)*(Fraction(v,3)-4)**2 for v,c in boot.items())}")
print("bootstrap numerator/3 ; multiplicity")
for num,count in sorted(boot.items()): print(f"{num}/3 ; {count}")
y = standard+new
nums = [2*sum(y[i] for i in a)-sum(y) for a in combinations(range(6),3)]
print(f"permutation allocations={len(nums)}; absolute two-sided p={sum(abs(v)>=12 for v in nums)}/{len(nums)}")
print(f"Python libraries: SciPy {scipy.__version__}")

22Reproduce null simulation and the coverage experiment

This second complete program uses NumPy and SciPy. Its first experiment approximates the same t₄ tail with three budgets and three declared seeds. Each row is a separate simulation from its seed, rather than a promise that runs with larger budgets are independent of smaller ones using the same seed. Within each run, generated studies are independent.

import numpy as np
from math import sqrt,ceil
from scipy.stats import t

observed = 4/sqrt(2/3)
p_exact = 2*t.sf(observed,4)
print("B seed hits plus_one MCSE_raw")
for B in [999,9999,99999]:
    for seed in [391,392,393]:
        rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
        z = rng.normal(size=(B,2,3))
        differences = z[:,0,:].mean(axis=1)-z[:,1,:].mean(axis=1)
        pooled = z.var(axis=2,ddof=1).mean(axis=1)
        stats = differences/np.sqrt(pooled*(2/3))
        hits = int((np.abs(stats)>=observed).sum())
        raw = hits/B
        print(f"{B} {seed} {hits} {(hits+1)/(B+1):.6f} {sqrt(raw*(1-raw)/B):.6f}")
print(f"theoretical p={p_exact:.9f}")
# Coverage experiment, not a validation of any real company model.
M,K = 2000,499
rng = np.random.default_rng(394)
z = rng.normal(size=(M,2,3))  # independent Normal(0,1) groups, true delta=0
estimate = z[:,0,:].mean(axis=1)-z[:,1,:].mean(axis=1)
se = np.sqrt(z.var(axis=2,ddof=1).mean(axis=1)*(2/3))
critical = t.ppf(.975,4)
covered_t = int((np.abs(estimate)<=critical*se).sum())
indices = rng.integers(0,3,size=(M,K,2,3))
resampled = np.take_along_axis(z[:,None,:,:],indices,axis=3)
boot = np.sort(resampled[:,:,0,:].mean(axis=2)-resampled[:,:,1,:].mean(axis=2),axis=1)
low,high = boot[:,ceil(.025*K)-1],boot[:,ceil(.975*K)-1]
covered_boot = int(((low<=0)&(high>=0)).sum())
print(f"coverage experiment M={M}; bootstrap draws per study={K}; seed=394")
print(f"t coverage hits={covered_t}; rate={covered_t/M:.6f}")
print(f"percentile coverage hits={covered_boot}; rate={covered_boot/M:.6f}")
print(f"outer MCSE near .95={sqrt(.95*.05/M):.6f}")
print(f"NumPy {np.__version__}")

23Report a primary analysis and explain the sensitivity results

A model-based report could state: the new group averaged 9 and the standard group 5 output units per hour, an estimated population mean difference 4. Under independent Normal samples with common unknown variance, the pooled t statistic is 4.899 with 4 degrees of freedom, two-sided p=.00805 and 95% interval[1.733,6.267]. These are model-based population comparisons, not proof of a causal procedure effect.

The sensitivity discussion should say why other outputs differ. The conditional exchangeability test on the 20 pooled-label allocations has absolute two-sided p=.10 and coarse finite resolution. The exact empirical-bootstrap percentile interval is[2.667,5.333], but enumeration does not certify 95% coverage at three observations per group. The pivotal Monte Carlo results approximate the t reference within numerical uncertainty.

24Practice comparison, diagnosis and transfer with complete solutions

Attempt each problem before opening its worked solution. Begin by writing the estimand, data-generating or assignment mechanism and reference law. When a calculation is requested, show its inputs and units. When a method is invalid, explain why and propose a scientifically justified next step rather than merely increasing a simulation budget.

A. Reconstruct the common estimate

For new[8,9,10] and standard[4,5,6], calculate both means, both sample variances, pooled SE and t statistic. State units and assumptions.

Complete worked solution

New sum 27 gives mean 9; standard sum 15 gives mean 5. Thus δ̂=4 output units per hour. Each group has squared deviations 1+0+1=2 and denominator 3−1=2, so each sample variance is 1 in squared output units.

Pooled variance is(2×1+2×1)/4=1. The difference SE is √(1/3+1/3)=.816496581 units per hour. Dividing 4 by this SE gives t=4.898979486, dimensionless, with 4 pooled degrees of freedom.

The exact t reference requires independent Normal samples with common positive population variance. Equal observed sample variances do not establish that model. The descriptive difference remains 4 without it, but its t calibration requires that probabilistic justification. No causal effect is identified solely by this arithmetic.

B. Match a test to its interval

Use c=2.776445105 and SE .816496581 to reconstruct the 95% t interval. Why does it agree with the two-sided t test but not necessarily a permutation test?

Complete worked solution

The margin is 2.776445105×.816496581≈2.266957936, so the interval is[1.733042064,6.266957936]. It excludes 0, matching the t p-value .008049893 below .05.

Both constructions use the same pivot and reference: |(δ̂−δ₀)/SE|≤c rearranges to δ₀ inside these bounds. The positive denominator permits multiplying the inequality without changing its direction. This algebra establishes their duality under the declared convention.

The conditional permutation test uses a different finite reference and returns .10. No duality with this t interval was established. Report its conditioning and finite resolution rather than treating the mismatch as an arithmetic failure. Confidence is a repeated-procedure statement, not a posterior probability for the observed endpoints.

C. Repair a simulation of the t pivot

An analyst generates Normal(0,1) groups but divides every simulated mean difference by the observed SE. Why is that not the t simulation, and what should be recomputed?

Complete worked solution

The t reference includes randomness of the estimated pooled scale. Holding the observed denominator fixed removes that randomness and constructs another statistic. In standardized generator coordinates, the observed physical-unit SE is also not a substitute for each generated dataset's own scale.

For every study, calculate its two sample means, its two ddof 1 variances, pooled variance and √(sp²×2/3). Divide that study's difference by that study's SE, then compare |tb| with 4.898979486. This reproduces the full pivot.

Generating at common mean 0 and variance 1 is justified because location and scale cancel under the Normal null. It is not a general license to ignore unknown nuisance parameters for any statistic. State the invariant argument before using a convenient generator.

D. Quantify Monte Carlo precision

At B=9999 and seed 392 the null simulation has 74 exceedances. Calculate raw and corrected p-values and raw MCSE. What changes if B is multiplied by 100?

Complete worked solution

The raw tail estimate is 74/9999≈.007400740. The rank value is 75/10000=.0075. Raw MCSE is √[(74/9999)(1−74/9999)/9999]≈.000857. These quantities concern numerical approximation to theoretical p .008049893.

At a fixed underlying tail, multiplying B by 100 reduces the simulation SE scale by about 10. The realized point estimate still fluctuates and need not move monotonically toward the target. Increasing B does not change the six original observations or the SE of the population contrast.

For a calibrated Monte Carlo rank test, the observed and null-generated statistics need the exchangeability described in the guide. For reporting a fixed-tail estimate, distinguish the raw estimator's numerical uncertainty from the corrected rank value. Neither is a posterior probability that the scientific null is true.

E. Recover discrete bootstrap quantiles

Use cumulative counts 7 at 7/3,28 at 8/3,701 at 5 and 722 at 16/3 among 729 resamples. Find the inverse-CDF 95% percentile interval and explain exactness.

Complete worked solution

For the lower endpoint,7/729≈.00960 is below .025 and 28/729≈.03841 reaches it, so q.025=8/3. At the upper endpoint,701/729≈.96159 is below .975 and 722/729≈.99040 reaches it, giving q.975=16/3.

The interval is[2.666666667,5.333333333]. It follows the explicitly stated smallest-value-with-CDF-at-least-a rule, not a different interpolated quantile convention. All ordered resample multiplicities must be included.

Exact enumeration eliminates Monte Carlo uncertainty from this empirical distribution. It does not make the empirical populations identical to unknown populations or guarantee 95% repeated-sampling coverage. A numerically exact percentile calculation can remain an imperfect inferential approximation, especially at three observations per group.

F. Explain two valid p-values

Under the common-variance Normal null, explain how t p .00805 and conditional permutation p .10 can both be valid. Should they be averaged?

Complete worked solution

The t law calibrates over fresh independent Normal samples, including new pooled values and scale estimates. The permutation law fixes this pooled list and varies 20 equiprobable labels. Under the Normal equal-mean/common-variance null, equal full distributions justify that conditional exchangeability.

Both procedures can satisfy their relevant level guarantee while making different decisions for one observed sample. The absolute permutation tail has minimum .10 here, whereas the continuous unconditional t reference has finer values. Exact calibration does not make p-values unique across conditioning schemes.

Averaging p-values has no automatic validity justification. Select a primary method from the study's target, design and justified assumptions, and explain the alternative as a sensitivity comparison. Their disagreement is not an estimate of the probability that one model or one software package is correct.

G. Reject a false bootstrap p-value

Every empirical-bootstrap difference is positive. An analyst reports p=0 because none of 729 contrasts is at most 0. Diagnose and repair the reasoning.

Complete worked solution

The bootstrap fitted two separate empirical populations retaining the observed difference 4. It was designed to approximate estimation uncertainty, not to impose equality of population means. Zero negative contrasts describe its support[2,6]. They do not provide a calibrated null tail or posterior null probability.

Define the scientific hypothesis and select an appropriate null reference. Under the declared Normal model, the t pivot or its correctly generated Monte Carlo reference tests δ=0. Under equal-distribution exchangeability, conditional permutation is another valid construction. A bootstrap test would need its own justified null centering or pivot.

Do not fix the claim merely by changing 0/729 to 1/730. The plus-one rank correction requires the appropriate null exchangeability; it cannot turn an estimation generator into a null generator. Repair the probability experiment before adjusting a numerical fraction.

H. Interpret a coverage experiment

With M=2000, t coverage 1896/2000 and percentile coverage 1635/2000, interpret results and distinguish M from K=499.

Complete worked solution

Rates are .948 and .8175. Under the generated independent Normal/common-variance model, the t procedure has theoretical coverage .95. Outer MCSE near that target is √(.95×.05/2000)=.004873, so .948 is unsurprising simulation variation.

The lower percentile rate warns about this n 3-per-group finite-K construction. It is not a proof about every bootstrap interval or every distribution. The output includes both empirical-population approximation and the 499-draw endpoint procedure used within each study.

M is the number of independent studies and controls precision of estimated coverage. K controls numerical approximation of each bootstrap interval. Raising M does not remove finite-K endpoint randomness; raising K does not eliminate uncertainty from finitely many studies. Broader claims require varying models and procedures, not merely attaching more decimal places to this rate.

I. Separate data scale, statistic and method changes

Convert output to units per minute. Which estimates, intervals and p-values change? Would replacing means by medians be the same kind of change?

Complete worked solution

Dividing every response by 60 divides the estimated difference, SDs, SE and interval endpoints by 60. The mean contrast becomes 4/60=1/15 units per minute. The t statistic is unchanged because its numerator and denominator scale equally. Its theoretical p-value is unchanged.

Permutation absolute ordering is unchanged by a positive common scale factor; its p remains .10. Every bootstrap difference and its quantiles scale by 1/60. The reported units must change consistently in tables, figures and interpretation.

Replacing means by medians is different: it changes the population target or statistic and may change ordering, reference laws and sensitivity. A unit conversion preserves the inferential contract after consistent scaling. A statistic change needs a new justification. Do not describe a scientifically different target as a harmless formatting update.

J. Repair an automated analysis of four randomized teams

Four teams of 20 workers are randomized two per procedure. Software uses 80 independent rows, reports a narrow bootstrap interval and selects the smaller of a t and permutation p-value. Give a complete correction plan.

Complete worked solution

Identify the team as the assignment unit. Individual workers in the same team must retain their common treatment label in a randomization reference. If two of four teams were chosen uniformly, the correct space hasC(4,2)=6 allocations. The 80 rows do not create 80 independent assignment decisions.

Define the target before choosing a statistic. Equal team sizes can make group averages of team means equal person-weighted averages for complete data; unequal sizes or missingness would distinguish them. For a sharp no-effect design test, keep whole-team outcomes fixed under the null and enumerate the six permitted allocations. Without actual team outcomes, no observed p-value can be calculated. A symmetric absolute contrast can have a minimum 2/6 when extreme complements are unique.

The independent-row bootstrap understates the dependence structure by pretending workers were independently sampled units. For population estimation, a team-level sampling model and an appropriate resampling procedure would need justification; with only two teams per arm, ordinary cluster bootstrap inference is itself fragile. Do not promise that preserving teams automatically creates a reliable interval.

Likewise, a pooled t reference based on 80 iid rows is unsupported. A team-level model may be considered with explicit assumptions, but it is not automatically exact design inference. Do not select the smaller p-value after observing results. Choose a primary justified analysis and explain alternatives as sensitivity checks.

Report unit, assignment, estimand or sharp null, statistic, reference, effect estimate and limitations. Distinguish numerical uncertainty from lack of independent teams. Plan more independent teams or a stronger design for confirmation; more iterations of an invalid generator cannot repair pseudoreplication.

Before continuing, explain why the theoretical t p-value and its pivotal Monte Carlo approximation should target the same tail, why the conditional permutation p-value need not match it, and why exact bootstrap enumeration does not certify confidence coverage. The final transfer problem requires preserving teams while separating estimation from sharp-null testing. If you cannot explain one link, revisit its example before relying on a software command.

25Keep the vocabulary tied to the random experiment

These terms summarize distinctions already used in the worked examples. They are not independent labels for competing software packages. In particular, numerical exactness, finite-model exactness and inferential approximation answer different questions. When reading an applied report, translate each technical term into what was fixed and what was repeated.

Estimand / estimando
Population or study quantity targeted by inference.
Pivot / pivote
Statistic with a reference law free of unknown nuisance quantities under stated conditions.
Nuisance parameter / parámetro accesorio
Unknown model quantity needed for the law but not the inferential target.
Conditional reference / referencia condicional
Probability law after fixing stated information, such as pooled values.
Empirical distribution / distribución empírica
Discrete fitted population assigning observed values their sample frequencies.
Coverage / cobertura
Repeated-study probability that an interval procedure includes the fixed target.
Monte Carlo SE / error estándar Monte Carlo

Bounds given by selected quantiles of an estimation bootstrap law.

Joint null law invariant under permitted label changes.
